# Plan: SNKRX in Atmos

Status: draft, not yet sent to the session that will execute it.

## Context

- SNKRX is an arcade roguelite by a327ex: a snake of heroes that
  attack by themselves, in an arena with waves of enemies, with a shop
  between levels. It sold well on Steam, and the author released the
  source in 2021 (code under MIT).
- It is written in Lua over LÖVE, 100% in the dynamic language: the
  game, and the author's own engine on top of LÖVE.
- This plan ports it to Atmos, over this compiler
  (`fsantanna/ceu-atmos`) and SDL2 (`lib/sdl.atm`), from scratch.
- There is a partial port by a student, to the reference Atmos over
  `pico-sdl`, with a written comparison of nine behaviors. It is
  imprecise as a port, but its structure and its documentation are a
  starting point.
- It follows the port of Pingus (`fsantanna/ceu-atmos-pingus`). The
  difference: the original is not C++ with state machines, but Lua
  with timers and callbacks. The study is about sequences written as
  nested callbacks, and the flags that guard them.
- Goals, in order:
    1. Behavior written as sequences: every timer callback of the
       original becomes an `await` in a task.
    2. No state variables for control: no flags that guard a phase, no
       timestamps kept to compute a cooldown.
    3. The same game in what can be compared: timings, formulas,
       content, and feel.
    4. Performance near the original, which runs on LuaJIT.

## Settled decisions

To confirm with the author before the work starts:

- **From scratch**, over `ceu-atmos` and SDL2. The student port is
  read, not converted: its task structure and its documents are
  reused, with credit.
- **Coordinates in pixels**, 480x270 as the original, scaled to the
  window. Not percentages.
- **Physics**: no Box2D. A small physics of its own (see Physics).
- **Fidelity**: by events and formulas, not tick by tick (see
  Fidelity).
- **Scope**: the whole game in two steps (see Scope). Out: Steam,
  achievements, the web build.
- **Engine**: one C header over SDL2 for shapes, text, sounds, and the
  broad phase of collisions. Everything else in Atmos.

## Patterns

What the original does, and what it becomes:

| Original | Atmos |
|----------|-------|
| `t:after(d, f)` | `await d`, then the body of `f` |
| `t:every(d, f, n, g)` | `loop _ in n { await d ... }`, then the body of `g` |
| `t:every(cond, f)` | `await` of the event that makes `cond` true |
| `t:tween(d, obj, target, ease, f)` | a `Tween` task, awaited |
| `t:cooldown(d, cond, f)` | `loop { await d ; await cond ; ... }` |
| a flag set by one callback and cleared by another | the block between two awaits |
| `self.dead` checked in every method | the task ends |
| `Group` with `update` and `draw` of each object | a pool of tasks, with `:tick` and `:draw` |
| an `action` callback given to a button | the button returns, the caller decides |
| `TransitionEffect{transition_action=...}` | `await Transition(...)`, then the next screen |

The five patterns of the Pingus study apply, and the student port
already names two more to document: pausing (`toggle`), and dynamic
collections (pools).

## State variables

- A table in `doc/state.md`, as in Pingus: every flag and timestamp of
  the original that the port covers, with what became of it.
- The student's table counts 29 phase variables in nine behaviors. The
  port extends the count to the whole game.

## Physics

The original uses Box2D through LÖVE for: bodies of units, enemies,
and projectiles; walls; restitution; collision and trigger callbacks;
impulses; and steering forces (seek, wander, separation).

- The port has its own physics, with the same vocabulary: circles and
  rectangles, velocity, damping, impulses, restitution on walls,
  separation between bodies.
- One broad phase per tick, in C, over all bodies. It emits an event
  to the two tasks of each contact that starts. No task scans the
  others (the lesson of Pingus).
- The steering of the engine (317 lines) is ported as it is.
- This is where the port departs from the original, by design. Two
  runs with the same seed will not move the same.

## Fidelity

Tick by tick is out of reach: positions come from Box2D, and choices
from 675 calls to the random generator. What is compared:

- **Timings.** Every delay of the original is a number in the source.
  A run with scripted input, a fixed seed, and a fixed tick prints its
  events (wave starts, markers, spawns, casts, level end) with their
  times, in the original and in the port.
- **Formulas.** Stats per level, damage, gold, interest, the odds of
  the shop: unit tests against values printed by the original.
- **Content.** The tables of characters, classes, passives, and levels
  are data, converted once and compared whole.
- **Distributions.** For what depends on chance (which elite, which
  card), many runs and a comparison of frequencies.
- **Feel.** Screenshots and short recordings side by side, judged by
  the author.

Set up on day one, as in Pingus:

- The original running in the container with a fixed seed and tick,
  with a patch that prints the trace (`tools/bench/original.patch`).
  It needs LÖVE and a virtual display, which need the author's
  permission to install.
- The test mode of the port with no screen: frames to run, scripted
  mouse, screenshots, a trace, no frame cap.

## Scope

### First version: a run, from the menu to the end of a level

- Menu, transition, shop, arena, outcome, and back.
- The snake: the head, the followers, death, promotion, bars.
- Ten characters, one of each kind of attack (projectile, area,
  nearest enemy, summon, buff, heal).
- Seekers, four elites, one boss.
- Waves, markers, gold, the first five levels.
- Sounds, text with colors, camera shake, hit effects.

### Second version: the game

- All 62 characters, their classes with set bonuses, and the passives.
- All elites and bosses, the 25 levels, the loops after the end.
- Options, save, pause.

### Out

- Steam, achievements, the web build, controllers.

## Benchmark

- Baseline: the original on LÖVE 11 (LuaJIT), in the same container,
  one after the other, with a fixed seed.
- Measure: peak memory, CPU in 10 s at 60 frames per second, frames in
  10 s with no cap, the time of a tick with many objects, the size of
  the program, lines and tokens against the Lua.
- Scenarios: the menu; the shop; level 1; a late level with hundreds
  of enemies and projectiles.
- This is the first measure of this compiler against LuaJIT. The
  runtime with trails is what it tests.

## What the port may need from the compiler

Report in `doc/compiler.md`. Known from Pingus and from the student
port:

- An await over a pool with a condition.
- `emit` to a level counted by hand (`emit @2`) is fragile: a way to
  name the receiver.
- Tweens and springs are written by hand: candidates for `lib/`.

## Risks

- **No exact fidelity.** Without Box2D and the same random calls, the
  port cannot prove itself tick by tick, as Pingus did. The comparison
  rests on timings, formulas, and the author's eye.
- **Size of the content.** 62 characters in 4000 lines, each with its
  attack. The first version is cut to ten.
- **The original in the container.** LÖVE needs OpenGL and a display.
  If it cannot run there, the trace of the original must come from a
  machine of the author.
- **Assets.** Sounds and fonts with licenses that may not allow a
  copy in the repository.
- **Performance.** LuaJIT is fast, and the original draws many small
  shapes: the drawing must be batched in C.

## Workflow

- Commits straight to `main`, small, each with its test.
- Decisions that belong to the author are asked one at a time, with a
  recommended option.
- Findings in the compiler are reported, not worked around in
  silence.
